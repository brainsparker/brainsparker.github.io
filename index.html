01 / Work

A few things I’m proud of.

Three messy product problems, what we changed, and what happened next.

01
You.com / AI search

Making search feel more like a conversation.

You.com began as a traditional search engine with a layer of AI tools. Research made the opportunity clear: people wanted to ask follow-ups and shape their intent in conversation. I led the move to a chat-first experience with answers grounded in sources.

Open the product notes
SignalPeople wanted direct answers and an easy way to keep refining what they meant.
DecisionBring the prompt, answer, sources, and conversational context into one clear experience.
ResultChat became the product’s main experience and its direction going forward.
1M → 10MMonthly users as chat became the main experience
Shipped interface / You.com
02
Crate & Barrel / Search infrastructure

Fixing the messy plumbing behind search.

Crate & Barrel’s search, inventory, and product data didn’t always agree. That made discovery frustrating for shoppers and expensive for the business. I led the rebuild underneath it all so the same trustworthy information powered every channel.

Open the product notes
SignalShoppers were seeing conflicting product and inventory information depending on where they looked.
DecisionBuild one reliable search foundation for every brand and channel.
ResultMore consistent discovery, more accurate fulfillment, and less manual cleanup.
7 figuresROI delivered in the first 30 days after launch
Commerce search / Crate & Barrel
03
G2 / Marketplace discovery

Helping buyers feel confident about a choice.

G2’s buyers had plenty of software options. The harder part was knowing which one was right. I owned the search roadmap across ranking, personalization, recommendations, and AI-powered guidance.

Open the product notes
SignalMore choices weren’t enough; buyers needed a clearer path through them.
DecisionMake search, recommendations, and AI guidance feel like one connected buying journey.
ResultBuyers explored categories and compared products with more confidence.

03 / How I think

Three ideas I keep coming back to.

Nothing fancy—just the standards I use to decide whether something is worth shipping.

01 / Good

Good should show up.

People should feel the value, reach it without a fight, and come back because they want to.

02 / Products

A product should make something better.

Features are the parts. The product is the change a customer can actually feel.

03 / End users

People always have a choice.

Build something useful enough that people choose it again, even when they have other options.
